Keyword Clustering in SEO: One Group, One Page

Keyword clustering in SEO means putting searches that share one need into one group, so that each group gets one page. The words can differ; what decides the group is whether one page would satisfy everyone who typed them.

Below, a raw list for an invented lawn-care company is folded into groups, then each group gets its fate: a page, a section, or nothing.

The raw list

Sixteen searches, in no order, the way they first arrive:

lawn mowing service          lawn aeration
cost to aerate lawn          lawn aerator rental
lawn care near me            when to aerate lawn
brown spots in grass         overseeding after aeration
dog urine spots on lawn      how much is lawn mowing
grub damage lawn             weekly lawn mowing price
lawn aeration service        best time to overseed
core aeration vs spike       lawn mowing company

Folded into groups

Group (the need) Searches in it Becomes
Hire a mowing crew lawn mowing service, lawn care near me, lawn mowing company service page
Price of mowing how much is lawn mowing, weekly lawn mowing price price page
Hire aeration lawn aeration service, lawn aeration service page
Price of aeration cost to aerate lawn section on the aeration page
Timing when to aerate lawn, best time to overseed, overseeding after aeration one seasonal guide
Compare types core aeration vs spike section on the aeration page
Fix a patchy lawn brown spots in grass, dog urine spots on lawn, grub damage lawn one guide, three sections
Rent equipment lawn aerator rental no page

Sixteen searches, five pages. Without grouping, the same list tempts a site into ten or more thin pages that compete with each other.

Three tests for "one group or two?"

1. Would one page answer both fully? "When to aerate lawn" and "best time to overseed" share a season and a visit, so one guide covers both.

2. Is the person doing the same thing? "Lawn aeration service" and "lawn aerator rental" share two words, but one wants to hire and the other wants to do the job with rented gear. A lawn-care company has nothing to sell the second person, so that group gets no page.

3. Is the answer big enough to stand alone? "Cost to aerate lawn" is a real need, but the answer is a few lines. It lives as a section on the aeration page instead of a thin page of its own.

What makes a group trustworthy

  • You can open it. Each group lists the searches inside it. A group name with nothing behind it is a claim, not a result.
  • It has a count. Monthly searches beside each group show which pages matter most.
  • It has a name a customer would use. "Fix a patchy lawn", not "turf remediation".

How many people look for this

About 390 people in the US search "keyword clustering" each month, and 110 search "keyword grouper". Another 590 search "keyword clustering tool". Whichever way the grouping gets done, the test of the result stays the same: one group, one need, one page.
