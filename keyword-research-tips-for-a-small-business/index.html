Keyword Research Tips for a Small Business

The most useful keyword research tip for a small business: aim at the searches a buyer types, even when few people type them. A monthly count tells you how big a pool of searchers is, not how many customers are in it.

The seven tips below follow from that. Each comes with one line for the same example business, so you can see it applied.

The example: a two-person bookkeeping firm in Milwaukee that works only with restaurants.

1. Small searches with a buyer behind them pay

A phrase typed by twenty people a month can beat one typed by two thousand, if the twenty are ready to hire.

For the bookkeeper: "bookkeeping for restaurants" brings owners looking for help. "What is bookkeeping" brings students. One new client pays for months; a thousand students pay nothing.

2. Read a count as a size, not a promise

A phrase with 880 searches a month does not send 880 visits to anyone. The count is the size of the pool. How much of it you reach depends on your page, your competitors and who the searchers are.

For the bookkeeper: compare counts between phrases to see which wording is more common. Do not turn a count into a visit forecast.

3. Start from the jobs you want, in the customer's words

Write down the work you want more of. Then write how a customer would ask for it. Owners rarely use the trade's terms.

For the bookkeeper: a restaurant owner types "catch up on my books" or "help with restaurant payroll", not "monthly reconciliation services".

4. Group the wordings of one need before ranking

People ask for one thing in many ways. "Restaurant bookkeeper", "bookkeeping for restaurants" and "restaurant accounting help" are one need and want one page. Their counts overlap, because the same people type several of them, so do not add them up as if they were separate customers.

For the bookkeeper: one page for restaurant bookkeeping, not three thin pages for three wordings.

5. Put a customer's value next to every count

Ask what one customer is worth over a year, and how many enquiries turn into one customer. A need worth a lot per customer can live on a few dozen searches a month.

For the bookkeeper: a client on a monthly fee for three years is worth far more than a one-time tax job. Rank the monthly-client needs first.

6. Check the country and the season

A US count and a worldwide count for the same phrase can be far apart. And a monthly figure is often a long average, so a busy season looks flat.

For the bookkeeper: searches for year-end and tax help rise in winter. Have those pages ready before the rise, not during it.

7. Look for needs your competitors leave open

A need with steady searches that none of your local competitors has a page for is the easiest page to win.

For the bookkeeper: if no firm in town has a page about bookkeeping for food trucks, that page has the field to itself.

The seven tips on one card

Copy this and keep it next to your list of phrases:

[ ] Does a buyer type this, or a learner?
[ ] Am I reading the count as a size, not a promise?
[ ] Is it in the customer's words, not the trade's?
[ ] Have I grouped it with the other wordings of the same need?
[ ] What is one customer from this need worth?
[ ] Which country and which months is this count for?
[ ] Do my competitors have a page for it?
